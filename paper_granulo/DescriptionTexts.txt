1 (Fig. 1) - Four panels in a row, each with a verdict below. (a) A pixelated digital disk of radius 5 with eight red pixels marked by crosses near the diagonals: no translate of the radius-4 disk inside covers them; verdict round yes, granulometry no. (b) The same disk with these pixels in blue, recovered by the opening with the disk itself; verdict round yes, granulometry yes. (c) A small plot of roundness error against radius up to 48: the octagon chain grows linearly to about 1.9 pixels, the digital disks stay near 0.2; verdict round no, granulometry yes. (d) A fan of all lattice directions of length at most 3, with shaded wedges next to the horizontal and vertical axes that contain no direction; below, the bound error at least about rho over 16 pi squared delta squared.
2 (Fig. 2) - Three line plots of error against radius. (a) Radius up to 48, six families: Gauss disks stay near 0.2, the periodic-line and DSS chains reach about 1, octagons about 1.9 and Bresenham chains about 3.2. (b) Log-log, radius up to 3000: the chains of Theorem 2 with m equal to 2 and 8, each lying between its dashed upper bound and dotted lower bound. (c) Log-log: the multiscale chain stays just below 0.3 times the square root of the radius.
3 (Fig. 3) - Three panels. (a) A binary image of a few overlapping disk-shaped particles; the opening by the disk of radius 12 is shown in dark grey, and pixels kept by the opening with radius 13 but removed by the opening with radius 12 are marked red along the boundary of two merged particles. (b) Bar chart of the pattern spectrum against radius 0 to 14: grey bars for single disk openings, three of them negative and drawn red and hatched, and black bars for the sup-closure, all non-negative. (c) Line plot of the spread of the size distribution over rotation angles against radius 0 to 40: the sup-closure stays below 0.03, the 13-direction periodic chain mostly low with a few peaks, octagons up to 0.22.
