1 (Fig. 1) - Three panels. (a) A pixelated disk of radius 5 drawn as grey squares; eight pixels near the diagonals are marked red with white crosses, showing pixels that no translate of the radius-4 disk fitting inside can reach. (b) A fan of line segments for all lattice directions of length at most 3; shaded wedges next to the horizontal and vertical axes contain no direction. (c) An arc of a circle with two polygon edges tangent to it; their common vertex q lies outside the circle, above a shaded wedge of normal directions of angle at least alpha.
2 (Fig. 2) - Two line plots. (a) Error in pixels against radius up to 48 for six families: Gauss disks stay near 0.2, the periodic-line and DSS chains reach about 1, octagons about 1.9 and Bresenham chains about 3.2. (b) Log-log plot of error against radius up to 3000 for the chains of Theorem 2 with m equal to 2, 4 and 8, each lying between its dashed upper bound and dotted lower bound, and a multiscale chain following 0.3 times the square root of the radius.
3 (Fig. 3) - Four small plots of the fraction of area surviving opening against radius from 0 to 40, one curve per rotation angle from 0 to 45 degrees. For single Gauss-disk openings and their sup-closure the curves almost coincide; the single openings show small increases. For octagons the curves spread visibly; for the 13-direction periodic chain they spread much less.
