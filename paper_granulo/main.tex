\begin{abstract}
Granulometries by disks are the standard morphological tool for measuring size distributions. On $\Z^2$ they face a conflict: the structuring elements should look like Euclidean disks, and they should satisfy Matheron's absorption axiom, i.e.\ each disk must be open with respect to every smaller one. We first show that the digital disks of common libraries violate the axiom for most pairs of radii, so that size distributions computed with them can increase with the radius. Families that satisfy the axiom are classically built as Minkowski chains of small structuring elements, such as neighbourhood sequences, periodic lines or Bresenham decompositions, and they are visibly polygonal. We prove that this is unavoidable. If the mean radius of a Minkowski chain grows by at most $\delta$ per element, then every element of radius $\rho$ lies at Hausdorff distance at least $\rho/(16\pi^2\delta^2)$ from every disk, asymptotically. Hence fine granulometric chains are linearly anisotropic. An explicit chain of periodic lines attains this rate up to a constant factor of~4, and letting $\delta$ grow like $\rho^{1/4}$ yields error $\Theta(\sqrt\rho)$. The lower bound extends to $\Z^n$, and for a fixed library its constant is computed exactly by linear programming and is attained by octagons. Experiments on synthetic and real images illustrate both the violation of the axiom by digital disks and the anisotropy of granulometric chains.
\keywords{Granulometry \and Digital disk \and Minkowski sum \and Lattice polygon \and Width function \and Pattern spectrum}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Granulometries, introduced by Matheron~\cite{matheron1975}, formalise sieving: a family of openings $(\gamma_\lambda)_{\lambda\ge0}$ that are decreasing in $\lambda$ and satisfy the absorption law $\gamma_\lambda\gamma_\mu=\gamma_\mu\gamma_\lambda=\gamma_{\max(\lambda,\mu)}$. The size distribution and pattern spectrum derived from them~\cite{maragos1989,serra1982,soille2003} are among the most widely used morphological descriptors. For openings by structuring elements $B_\lambda$, absorption holds if and only if $B_\mu$ is $B_\lambda$-open, i.e.\ a union of translates of $B_\lambda$, for all $\lambda\le\mu$. Euclidean disks satisfy this condition, and they form the canonical isotropic granulometry.

On the grid $\Z^2$, practitioners face a choice that is rarely made explicit. The digital disks $\{p\in\Z^2:\|p\|\le r\}$ of common software are round, but they are \emph{not} open with respect to each other (Sect.~\ref{sec:violation}). With \texttt{scikit-image}~\cite{vanderwalt2014}, $73\%$ of the pairs of radii up to $40$ violate the condition, and size distributions computed with these disks increase with the radius on most random test images. The classical remedy~\cite{adams1993,jones1996,soille2003,vincent1994} builds the structuring elements as \emph{Minkowski chains} $D_k=D_{k-1}\oplus C_k$, which are automatically granulometric. Their elementary sets $C_k$ come from small libraries: the $4$- and $8$-neighbourhoods give octagons (neighbourhood sequences~\cite{rosenfeld1968,farkas2006}), and there are also periodic lines~\cite{jones1996} and Bresenham segments~\cite{adams1993}. These families are correct but polygonal.

We show that this trade-off is a theorem, and we determine its rate.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lower bound} (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}, Corollaries~\ref{cor:mean}--\ref{cor:library}). If the mean radius of a Minkowski chain grows by at most $\delta$ per element, then \emph{every} element of radius $\rho$ has error $\varepsilon\ge(1-o(1))\,\rho/(16\pi^2\delta^2)$, where the error is the Hausdorff distance to the nearest Euclidean disk. Every fine chain is therefore \emph{linearly} anisotropic. If instead the fitted radius grows by bounded steps, then the maximal error is at least $c\,\rho^{1/3}$. For a fixed finite library the slope $\kappa/(1+\kappa)$ is given by a linear program, and octagons attain it.
\item \textbf{Matching upper bound} (Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}). An explicit chain of periodic lines along the boundary of the square $[-m,m]^2$ has steps $\delta\le\sqrt2m/\pi$ and error $\varepsilon\le\rho/(8m^2)+2m^2$. The optimal error is thus $\Theta(\rho/\delta^2)$ for $\rho\gtrsim\delta^4$, with constants within a factor of 4 asymptotically, and $\Theta(\sqrt\rho)$ when $\delta\sim\rho^{1/4}$.
\item \textbf{Higher dimensions} (Theorem~\ref{thm:zn}). In $\Z^n$, $\varepsilon\ge c_n\,\rho\,\delta^{-2n}$: bounded steps force linear anisotropy in every dimension.
\item \textbf{Evidence.} We quantify the violation of the axiom by standard digital disks (Table~\ref{tab:violation}), compare practical families (Table~\ref{tab:families}), and test size distributions on rotated synthetic and real images (Table~\ref{tab:rotation}).
\end{itemize}
The message for practice is that one can be correct (granulometric) or round, but not both at all scales, and our bounds quantify the price. Several lines of related work bear on this problem.
\begin{itemize}
\item Chamfer norms~\cite{borgefors1986,thiel2001} approximate Euclidean balls by polygons with a fixed set of edge directions. When their balls form Minkowski chains, as for $3\times3$ masks, Corollary~\ref{cor:library} applies to them.
\item Neighbourhood sequences~\cite{farkas2006} and zonohedra~\cite{jensen2019} optimise the roundness of a fixed library.
\item Soille and Talbot~\cite{soille2001} noted that radial decompositions by Bresenham lines do not generate granulometries.
\item Normand~\cite{normand2003} builds convex structuring elements by unions of translates instead of Minkowski sums, a construction outside the scope of our bound.
\end{itemize}
To our knowledge, no lower bound of this kind has been published.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
For a finite set $X\subset\Z^n$ and a unit vector $u$, let $h_X(u)=\max_{p\in X}\langle p,u\rangle$ be the support function and $w_X(u)=h_X(u)+h_X(-u)$ the \emph{width function}, i.e.\ the width of $\conv X$ in direction $u$. Both are additive under Minkowski addition~\cite{schneider2014}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:additive}
h_{X\oplus Y}=h_X+h_Y,\qquad w_{X\oplus Y}=w_X+w_Y .
\end{equation}
The \emph{anisotropy} of $X$ is $\A(X)=\max_uw_X-\min_uw_X$, and its \emph{mean radius} is $\bar r(X)=\frac12\int w_X\,d\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the uniform probability measure on the sphere; $\bar r$ is additive as well. We say that $X$ is \emph{$\varepsilon$-round with radius $\rho$} if $\dH(\conv X,B(c,\rho))\le\varepsilon$ for some centre $c$. Then $|h_X(u)-\langle c,u\rangle-\rho|\le\varepsilon$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round}
2\rho-2\varepsilon\le w_X\le2\rho+2\varepsilon,\qquad \A(X)\le4\varepsilon,\qquad|\bar r(X)-\rho|\le\varepsilon .
\end{equation}
The opening of $Y$ by a structuring element $D$ is $Y\circ D=\bigcup\{D+t: D+t\subseteq Y\}$. A family $(D_k)$ generates a granulometry if and only if $D_l\circ D_k=D_l$ for all $k\le l$~\cite{matheron1975,serra1982}; since the relation is transitive, consecutive elements suffice. A \emph{Minkowski chain} is a family with $D_k=D_{k-1}\oplus C_k$, and every Minkowski chain is granulometric. Its \emph{increments} are the $C_k$, its \emph{steps} are $\bar r(D_k)-\bar r(D_{k-1})=\bar r(C_k)$, and, if all $C_k$ belong to a finite set $\mathcal L$ (up to translation), $\mathcal L$ is its \emph{library}.

\section{Digital Disks Are Not Granulometric}\label{sec:violation}
We tested the condition ``$D_r$ is $D_s$-open'' for all pairs $1\le s<r\le40$, for the digital disks of \texttt{scikit-image}~\cite{vanderwalt2014} (\texttt{disk}, the Gauss digitisation $\{p:\|p\|\le r\}$) and of OpenCV~\cite{bradski2000} (\texttt{MORPH\_ELLIPSE}). As Table~\ref{tab:violation} shows, most pairs violate it, including most consecutive pairs. When $D_r$ is not $D_s$-open, $X\circ D_r\not\subseteq X\circ D_s$ for some $X$, and the size distribution $r\mapsto|X\circ D_r|$ can \emph{increase}, so the pattern spectrum takes negative values. On random $120\times120$ images made of 3 to 11 disks, rectangles or digital disks ($r\le15$), this happens for 63\% (scikit-image) and 60\% (OpenCV) of the images. For example, in one image the opened area grows from 2053 to 2089 pixels between $r=8$ and $r=9$, and \texttt{scipy.ndimage}, \texttt{scikit-image} and OpenCV agree up to border conventions.

\section{Lower Bounds}\label{sec:lower}
Additivity~\eqref{eq:additive} turns the roundness of two elements of a chain into a constraint on the increments between them. All edges of the increments are lattice vectors. If the increments are short, these vectors leave a gap of directions around each axis, and a polygon whose edges avoid an angular interval cannot be round.

\begin{lemma}[direction gap]\label{lem:gap}
Let $D\ge1$. No nonzero $(a,b)\in\Z^2$ with $a^2+b^2\le D^2$ has a direction angle in $(0,\arctan(1/D))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A direction in that interval requires $a>0$ and $b\ge1$, hence $b/a\ge1/a\ge1/D$.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lower}
Let $(D_k)_{k=0}^K$ be a Minkowski chain in $\Z^2$ whose increments have diameter at most $D\ge1$. If $D_0$ is $\varepsilon_0$-round with radius $\rho_0$ and $D_K$ is $\varepsilon_K$-round with radius $\rho_K$, then, with $c=1-\cos\bigl(\frac12\arctan\frac1D\bigr)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
4(\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_K)\ \ge\ 2\,(\rho_K-\rho_0-\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_K)\,c .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1.} Every edge of $\conv C_k$ joins two lattice points, so it is a lattice vector of norm at most $D$.
\emph{Step 2.} Let $P=\sum_k\conv C_k$ and $Q=\frac12(P-P)$. By \eqref{eq:additive}, $W:=w_{D_K}-w_{D_0}=w_P=2h_Q$. The edges of the centrally symmetric polygon $Q$ are parallel to edges of the $\conv C_k$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} (applied to $\pm e$) none has a direction in $(0,\alpha)$, where $\alpha=\arctan(1/D)$. The normal angles in $(\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2+\alpha)$ therefore belong to the normal cone of a single vertex $q$ of $Q$: $h_Q(\theta)=|q|\cos(\theta-\theta_q)$ on an interval $I$ of length at least $\alpha$.
\emph{Step 3.} We may assume $s:=\rho_K-\rho_0-\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_K>0$, since otherwise \eqref{eq:bound} is trivial. By \eqref{eq:round}, $\min W\ge2s$, so $h_Q>0$ and $|\theta-\theta_q|<\frac\pi2$ on $I$. On an interval of length $\alpha$ within $(\theta_q-\frac\pi2,\theta_q+\frac\pi2)$, the function $\cos(\theta-\theta_q)$ varies by at least $1-\cos(\alpha/2)$: by at least that much if $\theta_q\in I$, and by at least $1-\cos\alpha$ otherwise, by monotonicity. With $|q|\ge\min h_Q\ge s$ this gives $\max W-\min W\ge2sc$.
\emph{Step 4.} Finally $\max W-\min W\le\A(D_K)+\A(D_0)\le4\varepsilon_K+4\varepsilon_0$ by \eqref{eq:round}.\qed
\end{proof}

Only the first and the last element enter the bound. For a chain starting from a single point ($\varepsilon_0=\rho_0=0$), \eqref{eq:bound} gives $\varepsilon_K\ge\rho_K\,c/(2+c)$, where $c=\frac{1}{8D^2}(1+O(D^{-2}))$: \emph{every} element is at least $(1-o(1))\,\rho/(16D^2)$ away from a disk. Different constraints on the steps bound $D$ in different ways.

\begin{corollary}[mean-radius steps]\label{cor:mean}
If a Minkowski chain starting from a point has mean-radius steps $\bar r(C_k)\le\delta$, then $D\le\pi\delta$, and every element of radius $\rho$ has error $\varepsilon\ge\rho\,c/(2+c)=(1-o(1))\,\rho/(16\pi^2\delta^2)$, with $c=1-\cos(\frac12\arctan\frac1{\pi\delta})$. In particular, granulometric chains with bounded steps have error growing linearly with the radius.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Cauchy's formula the perimeter of a planar convex set is $\pi$ times its mean width, and it is at least twice its diameter. Hence $\diam\conv C_k\le\frac\pi2\cdot2\bar r(C_k)\le\pi\delta$.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[fitted-radius steps]\label{cor:fitted}
If all elements of a Minkowski chain are $\varepsilon$-round with radii satisfying $\rho_k-\rho_{k-1}\le\Delta$, then $\varepsilon\ge(1-o(1))\,((\rho_K-\rho_0)/512)^{1/3}$ as $\rho_K\to\infty$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:additive} and \eqref{eq:round}, $w_{C_k}=w_{D_k}-w_{D_{k-1}}\le2\Delta+4\varepsilon$, so $D\le2\Delta+4\varepsilon$. If $\varepsilon$ stayed bounded, then so would $D$, and \eqref{eq:bound} would force $\varepsilon\to\infty$. Hence $D=4\varepsilon(1+o(1))$, and \eqref{eq:bound} with $\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon_K=\varepsilon$ becomes $8\varepsilon\ge(\rho_K-\rho_0)/(64\varepsilon^2)\,(1+o(1))$.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[fixed library]\label{cor:library}
Let $\mathcal L$ be a finite library containing a set with more than one point, and let
\[\kappa(\mathcal L)=\inf\Bigl\{\tfrac14\A\bigl(\textstyle\sum_C\lambda_Cw_C\bigr):\lambda\ge0,\ \min_u\textstyle\sum_C\lambda_Cw_C(u)=2\Bigr\}.\]
Then $\kappa:=\kappa(\mathcal L)>0$, and the last element of every chain with library $\mathcal L$ satisfies $\varepsilon_K\ge\bigl(\kappa(\rho_K-\rho_0-\varepsilon_0)-\frac14\A(D_0)\bigr)/(1+\kappa)$, i.e.\ $\varepsilon\ge\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}\rho-O(1)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $\lambda$ in the simplex, $f(\lambda)=\A(\sum\lambda_Cw_C)/\min\sum\lambda_Cw_C$ takes values in $(0,\infty]$: $\sum\lambda_Cw_C$ is the width function of the polygon $\sum\lambda_C\conv C$, which does not have constant width, because bodies of constant width are strictly convex~\cite{schneider2014}. Moreover $f$ is continuous where finite and tends to $\infty$ where the minimum vanishes, so it attains a positive minimum, and $\kappa=\frac12\min f>0$. For the chain, write $W=w_{D_K}-w_{D_0}=\sum_Cn_Cw_C$ and $s=\frac12\min W\ge\rho_K-\rho_0-\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_K$. Then $\A(W)\ge4\kappa s$ and $\A(W)\le4\varepsilon_K+\A(D_0)$.\qed
\end{proof}

\section{Matching Upper Bound and Higher Dimensions}\label{sec:upper}
The lower bound is attained, up to constants, by a simple explicit chain. For $m\ge1$, let $V_m$ contain one vector from each pair $\pm v$ of integer points on the boundary of $[-m,m]^2$. These are $4m$ vectors of norm at most $\sqrt2m$, and consecutive directions differ by at most $\arctan(1/m)\le1/m$, the largest gap being between $(m,0)$ and $(m,1)$. Let $Z$ be the polygon circumscribed to the unit disk whose edges have the directions of $\pm V_m$; it is centrally symmetric, hence $Z=\sum_{v\in V_m}[-\frac{\ell_v}2\hat v,\frac{\ell_v}2\hat v]$ with $\hat v=v/\|v\|$, and $1\le h_Z\le\sec(\frac1{2m})$. For $t\ge0$ set $n_v(t)=\lfloor t\ell_v/\|v\|\rfloor$ and
\[
D(t)=\bigoplus_{v\in V_m}n_v(t)\{0,v\},
\]
the Minkowski sum of $n_v(t)$ copies of the periodic line $\{0,v\}$~\cite{jones1996}. As $t$ grows, the counts increase. When several counts increase at the same $t$, we add the corresponding lines one at a time, so the elements form a Minkowski chain of periodic lines starting from $\{0\}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:upper}
This chain has mean-radius steps at most $\sqrt2m/\pi$, and every element is $\varepsilon$-round with some radius $\rho$, with $\varepsilon\le\rho/(8m^2)+2m^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A step adds one periodic line, whose mean radius is $\|v\|/\pi\le\sqrt2m/\pi$. Every element, including those created when ties are broken, satisfies $n_v=t\ell_v/\|v\|-\varphi_v$ for some $t$ and $\varphi_v\in[0,1]$. The centred support function of $\conv D=\sum_vn_v[0,v]$ is therefore $H=\sum_vn_v|\langle v,u\rangle|/2=t\,h_Z-\sum_v\varphi_v|\langle v,u\rangle|/2$. Since $\sum_v\|v\|\le4\sqrt2m^2$ and $\sec y-1\le y^2$ for $y\le\frac12$, we get $t-2\sqrt2m^2\le H\le t+t/(4m^2)$. The disk of radius $\rho=(\max H+\min H)/2\ge t-2\sqrt2m^2$ is thus within $\varepsilon\le t/(8m^2)+\sqrt2m^2\le\rho/(8m^2)+\sqrt2m^2+\frac{\sqrt2}4\le\rho/(8m^2)+2m^2$.\qed
\end{proof}

With $\delta=\sqrt2m/\pi$, Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} gives $\varepsilon\le\rho/(4\pi^2\delta^2)+\pi^2\delta^2$, while Corollary~\ref{cor:mean} gives $\varepsilon\ge(1-o(1))\,\rho/(16\pi^2\delta^2)$. For $\rho\ge4\pi^4\delta^4$, where the constant term is dominated, the best achievable error is therefore $\Theta(\rho/\delta^2)$, with constants within a factor of $8$, and of $4$ asymptotically. We can also concatenate chains with $m_j=2^j$, each run up to $t_j\propto16^j$ on top of the frozen previous ones; anisotropies and radii add. The result is a single chain with steps $O(\rho^{1/4})$ and error $O(\sqrt\rho)$, which is optimal for steps $O(\rho^{1/4})$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:mean}. Numerically (Fig.~\ref{fig:error}b), the fixed-$m$ chains have slope about $1/(18m^2)$, between the bounds $1/(32m^2)$ and $1/(8m^2)$, and the multiscale chain satisfies $\varepsilon\le0.28\sqrt\rho$ for $20\le\rho\le5\cdot10^4$.

Like all our bounds, Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} concerns convex hulls. Minkowski sums of periodic lines can leave isolated holes, as Jones and Soille's decompositions also do~\cite{jones1996}. Adding the $2\times2$ square to the chain reduces them to $0.2$--$2\%$ of the hull's lattice points in our tests, and holes do not affect the granulometric property.

\paragraph{Higher dimensions.} In $\Z^n$ the directions are not ordered, but a measure argument replaces Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}. We state the result with unspecified constants; its exponent is not sharp, since for $n=2$ it gives $D^{-4}$ instead of $D^{-2}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zn}
Let $(D_k)$ be a Minkowski chain in $\Z^n$, $n\ge2$, whose increments have diameter at most $D\ge1$, and let $D_0$ and $D_K$ be $\varepsilon_0$- and $\varepsilon_K$-round (with respect to Euclidean balls). There are constants $b_n>0$ such that \eqref{eq:bound} holds with $c$ replaced by $1-\cos\alpha_n$, where $\sin\alpha_n=1/(4b_n(2D+1)^n)$. Hence $\varepsilon_K\ge c_n(\rho_K-\rho_0-\varepsilon_0)D^{-2n}-\varepsilon_0$. If the mean-radius steps are at most $\delta$, then $D\le2\delta/\mu_n$ with $\mu_n=\Gamma(\frac n2)/(\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\frac{n+1}2))$, and bounded steps again force linear growth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[sketch]
The walls of the normal fan of $Q=\frac12(P-P)$ lie in the hyperplanes $e^\perp$ for the edges $e$ of the $\conv C_k$, i.e.\ lattice vectors with $\|e\|\le D$. There are at most $(2D+1)^n$ of them, and each band $\{u:|\langle u,e/\|e\|\rangle|<s\}$ of the sphere has measure at most $b_ns$. So some $u_0$ satisfies $|\langle u_0,e\rangle|\ge2\sin\alpha_n\|e\|$ for all $e$, and the geodesic cap of radius $\alpha_n$ around $u_0$ meets no wall. On this cap $h_Q(u)=\langle q,u\rangle$ is linear. Restricting it to the great circle through $u_0$ and $q$ gives a variation of at least $|q|(1-\cos\alpha_n)$, and Steps 3 and 4 are as before. Finally, a segment of length $L$ has mean width $\mu_nL$, and mean width is monotone under inclusion.\qed
\end{proof}

\section{Practical Families and Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
\paragraph{Families.} Table~\ref{tab:families} compares families for $\rho\le48$ with mean-radius steps between $1.2$ and $2.2$, i.e.\ about one element per integer radius. For the fixed libraries (Bresenham/DSS segments as in~\cite{adams1993}, periodic lines as in~\cite{jones1996}), we use the optimal weights $\lambda^\ast$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:library}. We move towards the multiplicities $\operatorname{round}(t\lambda^\ast)$ one elementary set at a time, choosing at each step the set that keeps the element roundest; the chains contain the $2\times2$ square. We also include a chain of individual DSS computed by mixed-integer programming (HiGHS~\cite{huangfu2018}), which minimises the maximal error over $r\le48$. All elements of these granulometric families were verified to be hole-free. The optimised chains halve the error of octagons and reach about a third of that of Bresenham decompositions, but they remain three times less round than Gauss disks. At these radii Corollary~\ref{cor:mean} only forces $0.06$--$0.14$ for the steps in the table. The lower bound is far from binding for small disks, and its force lies in the growth rate at large radii (Fig.~\ref{fig:error}b). Relaxing the step size helps, as Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} predicts through $D$: with radius steps up to $4.1$, the 13-direction periodic chain reaches $0.41$. Conversely, if multiplicities could be real, a monotone family of DSS with $\|v\|\le45$ would have error at most $0.43$ on a grid of radii up to $2000$, as we found by solving a linear program; we do not claim that this persists for all radii. Integrality is what Step~1 of the proof exploits.

\paragraph{Size distributions.} For each scene $X$ and angle $\theta\in\{0^\circ,5^\circ,\dots,45^\circ\}$ we compute $F_\theta(r)=|X_\theta\circ D_r|/|X_\theta|$, implementing openings by chain elements as cascades of erosions and dilations by their elementary sets. We report the rotation sensitivity $S=\operatorname{mean}_r\operatorname{std}_\theta F_\theta(r)$ and the number of increases $F_\theta(r+1)>F_\theta(r)$, which are impossible for a granulometry. The scenes are nine squares of sides 17 to 81 pixels, rasterised exactly after rotation ($r\le40$), and three real images from \texttt{scikit-image}: fluorescence microscopy of nuclei (\texttt{human\_mitosis}, $r\le15$), \texttt{coins} ($r\le30$) and a material texture (\texttt{gravel}, $r\le20$). The real images are smoothed, rotated with bilinear interpolation and thresholded at the Otsu level of the unrotated image.

Table~\ref{tab:rotation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:rotation} illustrate the dilemma of the title. On the synthetic scene, Gauss disks are the least orientation dependent but produce 17 increases, i.e.\ negative pattern-spectrum values. Octagons are correct but strongly orientation dependent, and the periodic-line chain divides their sensitivity by three. On the three real images, the families differ by at most $0.6\cdot10^{-3}$ (nuclei, gravel) and $2.2\cdot10^{-3}$ (coins), with no consistent ranking, i.e.\ within the effect of interpolation and thresholding; no increases occur there. Rounded, randomly oriented objects average out the anisotropy of the structuring element. The choice of family therefore matters most for polygonal or aligned structures. The violation of the axiom by Gauss disks, in contrast, is a correctness issue that can surface on any image (Table~\ref{tab:violation}).

\section{Conclusion}
Standard digital disks do not generate granulometries, and granulometric Minkowski chains cannot be uniformly round. With mean-radius steps $\delta$ their error is $\Theta(\rho/\delta^2)$, and $\Theta(\sqrt\rho)$ when $\delta\sim\rho^{1/4}$; bounded steps force linear growth in every dimension. For practice, one must choose between correct but anisotropic chains, whose loss the constant $\kappa$ quantifies, and round but incorrect digital disks. Our periodic-line chains halve the loss of octagons. Three questions remain open: the optimal rate when only the fitted radius has bounded steps, between $\rho^{1/3}$ and $\rho^{1/2}$; whether granulometries that are not Minkowski chains, where $D_l$ is merely a union of translates of $D_k$, can escape the bound; and matching upper bounds in $\Z^3$.
